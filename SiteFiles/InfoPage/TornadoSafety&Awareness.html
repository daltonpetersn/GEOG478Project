Tornado Safety Tips

NOAA: Tornado Safety

  • Seek shelter in a sturdy building or designated storm shelter.

  • Stay away from windows and doors.

  • If you are in a mobile home, abandon it and seek more secure shelter.

  • Listen to weather updates and follow local emergency instructions.

  • Have an emergency kit ready with essentials like food, water, and first aid supplies.

  • Check for local weather advisories and see if there are any tornado watches or warnings in the vicinity.

Damage Mitigation

GovPilot: Tornado Mitigation

Tornado mitigation refers to the efforts and strategies implemented to reduce the impact of tornadoes on communities. This includes building codes, community preparedness programs, and public awareness campaigns. The goal is to minimize damage and protect lives during tornado events.

Tornado Preparedness

  • Have a family emergency plan in place.

  • Know the safest place in your home to take shelter.

  • Practice tornado drills with your family.

  • Keep a battery-powered weather radio or app for updates.

  • Have an emergency kit ready with essentials like food, water, and first aid supplies.

  • If building a home ensure tornado resistant materials are used during construction; i.e. anchor bolts, roof straps, and window shutters. Also ensure a shelter area is easily accessible in the home.

Tornado Recovery

  • Check for injuries and provide first aid if necessary.

  • Stay away from damaged areas until authorities say it's safe.

  • Document damage for insurance purposes.

  • Contact local emergency services for assistance.

  • Help neighbors and community members in need.

  • Participate in community recovery efforts and support local organizations.

  • Stay informed about recovery resources and assistance programs.

Tornado Watch vs Tornado Warnings

NOAA: National Weather Service

Tornado Watch

Tornadoes are possible in and near the watch area. Review and discuss your emergency plans, take inventory of your supplies and check your safe room. Be ready to act quickly if a warning is issued or you suspect a tornado is approaching. Watches can be seen on the National Weather Service's Website. The watch area is typically large, covering numerous counties or even states.

Tornado Warning

A tornado has been sighted or indicated by weather radar. There is imminent danger to life and property. Move to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building. Avoid windows. If in a mobile home, a vehicle, or outdoors, move to the closest substantial shelter and protect yourself from flying debris. Warnings are issued by your local forecast office. Warnings typically encompass a much smaller area (around the size of a city or small county) that may be impacted by a tornado identified by a forecaster on radar or by a trained spotter/law enforcement who is watching the storm.

Tornado Emergency

This is the National Weather Service's highest alert. A violent tornado is in the vicinity that poses a major threat to human life and property, with catastrophic damage confirmed. Seek shelter immiedietly, call any family or friends in the vicnity and alert the to the imminent danger and shelter in place until the weather service has ensured it is safe to come out.

Determining Tornado Density

The Enhanced Fujita Scale (EF Scale) is used to rate the intensity of tornadoes based on the damage they cause. The scale ranges from EF0 (weakest) to EF5 (strongest).
